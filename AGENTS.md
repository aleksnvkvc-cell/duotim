# Duo Tim website

Use emoji for website interface icons, including new buttons and links. Use the gold flyer palette for emoji and the original full-color brand marks for Facebook and Instagram. Keep text labels for links and use accessible names for icon-only controls. Preserve the Duo Tim logo, product imagery, and technical drawings.

Use open sections, lists, or rows for website content. Avoid card and tile layouts with boxed backgrounds, rounded containers, or drop shadows. Keep buttons, form fields, and image controls recognizable and accessible.

## UI/UX Pro Max design workflow

Reference: https://github.com/nextlevelbuilder/ui-ux-pro-max-skill

Treat UI UX Pro Max as a design-review methodology, not as a runtime dependency. If its local skill files and scripts are installed in the development environment, consult its design-system and UX guidance before redesigning components. Do not claim the full skill is installed merely because this reference exists.

### Duo Tim design constraints
- Preserve the existing flyer-derived light-gray background, gold accents, decorative lines, and ALU/PVC title treatment. Do not introduce unrelated palettes or pink UI.
- Keep the logo and the symmetric yellow V-shaped line beneath it aligned across viewport sizes.
- Preserve the recognizable hero image presentation, avoiding black letterboxing around product photographs.
- Prefer open, clean typography and layouts rather than decorative cards or excessive visual effects.
- Keep existing contact phone numbers, messaging destinations, product categories, and social links functional. On the contact page, match the taskbar's icons and show phone numbers in their respective contact sections.
- Use real product photographs already approved for the site; avoid placeholder imagery.
- Optimize for mobile-first layouts, readable contrast, keyboard access, semantic HTML, and descriptive alt text.

### Performance and quality gates
- Avoid nonessential animations, heavy animation libraries, large render-blocking assets, and layout shifts.
- Use responsive image sizing, explicit dimensions, lazy loading for below-the-fold images, and appropriate modern image formats when compatible.
- Keep the hero/LCP image prioritized rather than lazy-loaded.
- Check layouts at 360px, 768px, and 1440px and verify navigation, contact links, galleries, and forms.
- Before committing UI changes, review consistency, spacing, hierarchy, responsiveness, accessibility, and performance. Prefer targeted improvements over full rewrites.
